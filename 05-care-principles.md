Section B Q16 (4 marks) asks you to assess a scenario against the **CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance**:

| | Principle | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | Collective benefit | Does the use of the data benefit the community it came from? |
| **A** | Authority to control | Do the communities have a say in how their data is accessed and used? |
| **R** | Responsibility | Are relationships with those communities kept up over time? |
| **E** | Ethics | Are the communities' rights and wellbeing the main concern? Were they consulted about benefits, harms and future use? |

The practice exam answer key calls C "Community Benefit", while the official CARE wording is "Collective Benefit". Either name is fine. The marks come from your reasoning.

## Practice exam question

![Section B · Q16](https://img.shields.io/badge/Section_B-Q16-8250df?style=flat-square) ![4 marks](https://img.shields.io/badge/4_marks-57606a?style=flat-square)

AIBS holds genetic data from more than 5,000 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander participants. The study improved health checks across the country. The original contact people have all left, participants find it hard to withdraw their consent, and Indigenous health organisations find it difficult to get access to the data. Does AIBS comply with each principle?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- **C: met.** The data improved health outcomes in hundreds of hospitals.
- **A: not met.** The communities no longer have access to the data or any say in how it is managed and used.
- **R: not met.** Relationships weren't kept up after the data was collected, and all the contact people have gone.
- **E: not met.** There's no sign the communities were consulted about benefits, harms and future use, so the data could be used in ways that go against the rights of the people it came from.

To get the marks, name the principle, say whether it is met, and point to the specific part of the scenario that shows it.

</details>
